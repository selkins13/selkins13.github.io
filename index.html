The opportunity

Move from status collection to decision support.

Operational reporting often tells teams what happened, but not where to focus next. I shaped this concept around the questions leaders and delivery teams repeatedly need to answer: What is moving? What is aging? Where is risk accumulating? Who needs support?

Product thinking

Organized the experience around actions and exceptions instead of exposing a dense data model. Progressive filters preserve a useful default view.

Technical architecture

A small, typed-data-inspired JavaScript model drives KPIs, charts, stage distribution, and attention states from one source of truth.

Automation & quality

Reusable calculations reduce manual reporting work. Explicit empty states, semantic tables, keyboard controls, and deterministic sample data make the experience testable.

Data safety

Every organization, person, project, date, and metric is fictional. The interface uses common lifecycle concepts without reproducing private terminology or schemas.

The opportunity

Make tradeoffs visible before they become fire drills.

Resource planning can become a spreadsheet negotiation. This concept gives planners a fast portfolio view while keeping the individual context—skills, assignments, constraints, and upcoming availability—one click away.

Product thinking

Paired aggregate signals with person-level detail so leaders can spot patterns without losing the human context behind the plan.

Technical architecture

Derived weekly states, summary cards, project allocations, and detail views from a normalized set of fictional people and assignments.

Responsible workflow

Status colors are reinforced with labels and values. The model frames utilization as a planning signal—not a performance score.

Business impact

The workflow supports earlier staffing conversations, clearer ownership, and more sustainable delivery without making unsupported outcome claims.

Case study 03 · Enablement product

Interactive onboarding playbook

A role-aware guide that helps new team members find the right task, understand the first-month journey, and learn the reasoning behind repeatable processes.

Open focused demo →

Fictional by design

The opportunity

Design onboarding as a journey, not a document dump.

New team members need context, sequence, and confidence—not just links. I structured this concept around moments in the first month and the real jobs a person is trying to complete, with role-aware guidance and searchable reusable processes.

Information architecture

Combined a time-based journey with a task-based library so people can learn sequentially or find an answer in the flow of work.

Documentation system

Guide cards follow a repeatable pattern: purpose, owner, estimated time, prerequisites, steps, and completion signal.

Accessibility

Semantic regions, visible focus states, labeled controls, keyboard-operable tabs, readable contrast, and reduced-motion support are built into the experience.

Scalable enablement

The modular content model supports consistent updates and distributed ownership while preserving one coherent experience.
